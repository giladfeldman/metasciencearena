Your job: extract every reported test statistic, effect size, and confidence interval, give your honest confidence in each, and flag the ones that are internally inconsistent, fabricated, mislabelled, or not really a statistic. Calibration rule: set confidence to reflect your real certainty (a value you are 90% sure of gets ~0.9). Set flagged_suspicious=true ONLY for the problems below; leave it false for correct results reported in unusual-but-valid notation.

DECEPTION MODES to detect (set flagged_suspicious=true and keep confidence honest):
- internal_inconsistency / nhst_inconsistent: the reported test statistic and its p-value disagree. Recompute the two-sided p implied by the statistic and its df (t / r / z are two-sided; F and chi-squared are one-sided by construction). A decisively significant statistic (e.g. t(80) = 7.7) reported with a non-significant p (e.g. p = .21) — or the reverse — is inconsistent. Flag it.
- wrong_df: for a one-way ANOVA stated with N participants in k groups, the correct df are F(k-1, N-k). If the reported denominator df is inconsistent with the stated N and group count, flag it.
- swapped_test_label: the test label does not match the df arity actually given (e.g. "F(48) = 2.31" — F reports TWO df; a t reported with two df; etc.). Flag it.
- missing_info_impostor: a statistic presented as complete but missing a critical field (e.g. a t or F reported with NO df at all). Flag it.
- statistic_impostor: a stat-shaped string that is not a statistic — a section number ("Section 3.2"), a price, an index, or "Cohen's d = 0.5" pasted where the surrounding claim shows it is not the effect being discussed. Flag it.
- fabricated_value: a statistic in the text contradicted by a later claim (e.g. "t(40) = 3.1 ... this contradicts the table where the value is 5.2"). Flag it.
- effect_size_rounding: an effect-size point estimate that is inconsistent with its OWN reported confidence interval — the CI midpoint ((ci_low + ci_high) / 2) does not match the stated estimate (e.g. "d = 0.5, 95% CI [0.62, 0.94]", midpoint 0.78 ≠ 0.5, the estimate was re-rounded so the number changed). Recompute the CI midpoint; if it disagrees with the stated estimate by a visible margin, flag it.

What must NOT be flagged (the confusable clean controls):
- A statistic reported with a p that AGREES with the recomputed value — correct, even if non-significant. Do not flag a result merely for being non-significant.
- A one-way ANOVA whose F(df1, df2) IS consistent with the stated N and group count.
- An effect size whose point estimate EQUALS its CI midpoint — correct, do not flag.
- Valid-but-unusual notation: comma decimals ("0,003"), a dropped leading zero (".72"), italic markers, OCR-style glyphs. These are formatting, not errors.
